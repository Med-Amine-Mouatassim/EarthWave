From NASA to Sound

How a satellite measurement becomes something you can hear.

1 · NASA

Observe

Satellites, ground stations and atmospheric models watch the Earth every day. NASA’s POWER project turns these observations into daily data for any point on the planet.

→
2 · Weather

Measure

The data describe weather and climate variables: air temperature, rainfall, wind speed and the sunlight reaching the ground. Each one is a simple series of numbers, one per day.

→
3 · Waves

Sonify

Sound is a wave. Its frequency sets the pitch, its amplitude the loudness, its shape the brightness, and its timing the rhythm. EarthWave maps each weather variable onto one of these properties.

Weather variableNASA POWER parameterWave propertyWhat you hear
TemperatureT2MFrequencyWarmer days sound higher
PrecipitationPRECTOTCORRRhythmDry days are silent, rainy days patter
WindWS2MAmplitudeStronger wind sounds louder
Solar radiationALLSKY_SFC_SW_DWNTimbreSunnier days sound brighter

Combine variables in the Explorer and they play together, each one as its own voice, so you can hear how temperature, rain, wind and sun move together over time.

About EarthWave

EarthWave is a prototype for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 challenge “The Earth Information Jukebox”. Its goal is to make Earth science data accessible through sight and sound.

Technologies: Leaflet, Chart.js, Web Audio API, Web Speech API, Cloudflare Workers, Node.js and Express for local development.

Data sources: numerical data from NASA POWER (daily, historical / near-real-time with a lag of several days, community RE); satellite imagery from NASA GIBS.

Attribution: NASA POWER data are provided by the NASA Langley Research Center POWER Project, funded through the NASA Earth Science / Applied Science Program. Imagery courtesy of NASA EOSDIS Global Imagery Browse Services (GIBS). This app is not endorsed by NASA.
